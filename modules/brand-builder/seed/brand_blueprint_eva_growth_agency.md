## 1. Audience — Segments, Where They Hang Out, What They Value

### 1.1 Segments

The online-business acquisition market ($100K–$1M deal range) comprises several distinct buyer personas:

| Segment | Profile | Deal Range | What They Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-funded searchers** | Individual operators using SBA loans, seller notes, and personal capital. Often post-MBA or career-transitioners. | $100K–$750K | Cash flow durability, owner-replacement paths, clean financials, SBA-eligible structures. |
| **Micro-PE operators** | Small funds or holdcos acquiring 1–10 businesses. Often former operators or searchers who've scaled. | $250K–$1M+ | Recurring revenue, margin resilience, AI-resilience, portfolio fit, documented SOPs. |
| **SaaS acquirers** | Operators buying bootstrapped SaaS on [Acquire.com](https://acquire.com) or [Flippa](https://flippa.com). Tech-native. | $50K–$1M | MRR quality, churn, code maintainability, organic CAC, AI-resilience of the product. |
| **E-commerce/FBA buyers** | Operators acquiring Amazon FBA, DTC brands, or content sites on [Empire Flippers](https://empireflippers.com) or Flippa. | $100K–$1M | Margin quality (50%+ rewarded), organic brand demand, repeat purchase rates, platform concentration risk. |
| **Agency buyers** | Operators acquiring service businesses with documented SOPs and low founder dependency. | $100K–$500K | Client retention, systematized delivery, low owner dependency, team stability. |

The buy-and-sell online business market was valued at an estimated **$33.9 billion in 2025**, projected to grow at a 14.5% CAGR to ~$100.1 billion by 2033 ([ProMarketReports](https://www.promarketreports.com/reports/buy-and-sell-online-business-310216)). **Confidence: med** (single market-research firm estimate; cross-check with Flippa's own deal data below).

## 2. Market Patterns — What's Working NOW (2025–2026)

### 2.1 The Durability Shift

The dominant 2025–2026 pattern in digital M&A is a **structural shift from revenue-as-valuation-anchor to profit quality, cash generation, and margin resilience**. [Flippa's Q2 2026 report](https://flippa.com/blog/digital-ma-market-trends-2026-q2/) states: "The valuation logic of 2021 no longer applies; a new framework centered on durability, defensibility, and AI resilience has taken its place." **Confidence: high.**

What gets discounted aggressively in 2026: businesses under 18 months old, paid-social-only acquisition, SEO-dependent content without direct audience, owner-reliant agencies, thin margins (the "10% ecommerce profile"), and platform concentration. **Confidence: high.**

### 2.2 AI Resilience as a Valuation Factor

Flippa now explicitly categorizes deals as **AI-native** (benefits from AI), **AI-resilient** (structurally protected), and **AI-exposed** (core value at risk of displacement). AI-exposed businesses are "marked down aggressively if the core value proposition is at risk of being replicated or displaced." Examples: a Tech Tutorial Site sold at 2.8x multiple due to AI Overview traffic headwinds; a Healthy Eating Blog at 2.4x due to SEO reliance without direct audience. **Confidence: high** (first-party deal data).

### 2.3 AI in M&A Deal Sourcing

AI-powered deal sourcing is gaining traction across the M&A industry. AI can "analyse thousands of potential targets against strategic criteria in hours rather than weeks" and due diligence document review times have been reduced by 60–80% ([Brain — AI for M&A guide](https://startbrain.ai/blog/ai-for-mergers-acquisitions-guide/)). **Confidence: med** (agency blog, not independently verified, but directionally consistent with broader industry adoption).

### 2.4 Founder-Led Content as Pipeline Engine

The most effective brand-building pattern in 2025–2026 B2B/acquisition marketing is **founder-led content on LinkedIn + X + newsletter**. LinkedIn touchpoints influence **35% of all new business deals** across tracked pipelines, per [Dreamdata's 2025 B2B attribution research](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/linkedin-content-strategy-b2b-conversion-secrets-drive-alex-alleyne-xaxif) (tracking hundreds of mid-market and enterprise accounts). Pipeline influence from organic content "begins compounding meaningfully around day 219 of consistent publishing" and revenue lock-in "begins crystallising around day 320." **Confidence: high** (attribution research, multiple sources cited).

### 2.5 Thought Leadership as Trust Currency

The [2025 Edelman-LinkedIn B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report](https://christopholivierconsulting.com/thought-leadership-statistics/) (7 markets, n=1,934) found: 95% of "hidden" decision-makers say strong thought leadership makes them more receptive to outreach; 64% trust it more than product sheets; 79% are more likely to champion a vendor with consistent high-quality thought leadership during RFP processes. **Confidence: high.**

The [2024 Edelman-LinkedIn report](https://www.consideredcontent.com/b2b-thought-leadership-effectiveness-what-the-data-says-in-2026/) (3,484 global executives) found: 73% of decision-makers trust thought leadership more than marketing materials; 60% are willing to pay a premium; 86% would invite an organization with strong thought leadership into the RFP process. **Confidence: high.**

### 2.6 Build-in-Public as Trust Strategy

Building in public is "not a content strategy. It is a trust strategy" — and in a market "where attention is scarce, capital is selective, and buyers evaluate founders before they evaluate products," it has become "one of the highest-leverage moves an early-stage founder can make" ([Seedscope](https://seedscope.ai/blog/building-in-public-the-founder-growth-strategy-that-compounds-over-time)). Non-technical founders building in public on LinkedIn and X see **2.4x higher engagement rates** on business-narrative posts vs. feature announcements. **Confidence: med** (single source, but directionally consistent with broader Edelman findings on authenticity).

### 2.7 Marketplace Specifics (2026)

- **Empire Flippers**: Full-service brokerage, ~91% rejection rate, $2K/month minimum net profit, 15% commission to $700K. 2,600+ deals closed, ~119-day average sale. ([ExitBid](https://exitbid.io/blog/empire-flippers-vs-acquire-com-2026), [Small Business Tools](https://www.smallbusinesstools.org/guides/where-to-sell-a-small-business)) **Confidence: high.**
- **Acquire.com** (formerly MicroAcquire): ~45% approval rate, buyer-side subscription model ($390/year+), 6–8% closing fee, 500K+ registered buyers, $2B+ verified funds. SaaS-leaning. ([ExitBid](https://exitbid.io/blog/empire-flippers-vs-acquire-com-2026), [Clef](https://tryclef.com/blog/best-websites-to-find-businesses-for-sale)) **Confidence: high.**
- **Flippa**: Largest marketplace by volume, 12,000+ deals/year, 400,000+ weekly active buyers, ~10% success fee, self-serve + managed options. ([SMBCompare](https://smbcompare.com/articles/best-marketplaces-buy-sell-online-business), [Flippa](https://flippa.com/blog/digital-ma-market-trends-2026-q2/)) **Confidence: high.**

## 4. Content Archetypes — Which Formats Convert

Ranked by relevance to Eva's audience and conversion potential:

### 4.1 Deal Teardowns (Highest Priority)
Analyze a real listing on Flippa/Acquire.com/Empire Flippers against Eva's 11-parameter scoring framework. Show exactly what passed, what failed, and why. This is the **proof-of-product** content archetype — it demonstrates the value of Eva's scoring in real-time.

Why it works: "Specific observations from commercial experience" is one of the top content formats for pre-call trust in B2B ([Hey Sid](https://www.heysid.com/resources/how-thought-leadership-builds-trust-before-the-first-sales-call)). The Edelman-LinkedIn research found the highest-quality thought leadership "references robust research and strong supporting data" (cited by 55% of decision-makers) and "offers concrete guidance and case studies" (43%) ([Considered Content](https://www.consideredcontent.com/b2b-thought-leadership-effectiveness-what-the-data-says-in-2026/)). **Confidence: high.**

### 4.2 Data/Proof Posts
Leverage Eva's dataset of 92 closed deals. Publish aggregate patterns: "What the 92 deals we've scored tell us about [AI-resilience / margin thresholds / moat durability]." This is **proprietary data** — the strongest authority signal available.

Why it works: "Generic industry statistics, cited by every competitor, are perceived as filler. What builds genuine trust and credibility are internal benchmarks, anonymized customer success stories, and proprietary patterns observed from the vendor's unique market position" ([MarketersIndex](https://marketersindex.com/redefining-b2b-thought-leadership-crafting-content-that-influences-executive-decisions/)). **Confidence: high.**

### 4.3 Build-in-Public
Share Eva's development journey: scoring iterations (v1 → v6), what the AI agent got wrong and how it was corrected, deal-audit results, and the operator's own acquisition learnings.

Why it works: Building in public produces "2.4x higher engagement rates on business-narrative posts compared to feature announcement posts" and is "one of the highest-leverage moves an early-stage founder can make" ([Seedscope](https://seedscope.ai/blog/building-in-public-the-founder-growth-strategy-that-compounds-over-time)). "Authority built in public is the permission structure for commercial conversations in private" ([LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/linkedin-content-strategy-b2b-conversion-secrets-drive-alex-alleyne-xaxif)). **Confidence: high.**

### 4.4 Contrarian Thesis
Challenge industry assumptions: "Why most SaaS multiples on Acquire.com are wrong," "The AI-resilience test 90% of buyers aren't running," "Why 'boring but durable' beats 'exciting but fragile.'"

Why it works: 86% of "hidden decision-makers" (internal influencers in finance, legal, operations) "prefer perspectives that challenge their assumptions over content that merely validates existing thinking" ([MarketersIndex](https://marketersindex.com/redefining-b2b-thought-leadership-crafting-content-that-influences-executive-decisions/)). 81% of decision-makers "value thought leadership that helps them uncover previously unrecognized challenges or opportunities." **Confidence: high.**

### 4.5 Frameworks
Publish named, repeatable frameworks: "The 11-Parameter Deal Scoring Framework," "The AI-Resilience Test," "The Buy-Box Builder." Give them proper names so they become referenceable.

Why it works: The Founder Funnel OS recommends "1 signature framework or named concept per quarter" as a Signal-stage KPI ([Forkoff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/founder-growth/founder-funnel-strategy)). Named frameworks create "a reputation that competitors cannot easily displace" — the benchmark is "a hundred and fifty posts over a year" to reach an authority position ([LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/linkedin-content-strategy-b2b-conversion-secrets-drive-alex-alleyne-xaxif)). **Confidence: high.**

### 4.6 Founder Lessons
Personal reflections on the acquisition journey, mistakes made, and what was learned. Less about Eva the product, more about the operator behind it.

Why it works: "Employee accounts drive 30% of a company's LinkedIn post engagement" and "Social media users increasingly tune out corporate accounts, seeking authentic connections instead" ([Seedscope](https://seedscope.ai/blog/building-in-public-the-founder-growth-strategy-that-compounds-over-time)). **Confidence: high.**

## 6. Awareness Loops — Repeatable Hooks + Distribution Motions

### 6.1 The Weekly Deal Teardown Loop
**Hook:** "I ran [listing name] through Eva's 11-parameter scoring model. Here's what it flagged."
**Distribution motion:** Post the teardown on X (Monday) → rewrite for LinkedIn (Tuesday, 48hr lag) → expand into newsletter deep-dive (Thursday) → pin as a reply-generating thread. End with: "Want me to run your target listing through the model? DM me."
**Why it loops:** Each teardown is self-contained, uses real data, and creates a natural CTA. The format is infinitely repeatable as long as new listings exist (which they always do — Flippa alone has 400,000+ weekly active buyers ([Flippa Q2 2026](https://flippa.com/blog/digital-ma-market-trends-2026-q2/))).

### 6.2 The Market Pattern Loop
**Hook:** "I've scored 92 deals. Here's the pattern I see in [AI-resilience / margin quality / owner dependency]."
**Distribution motion:** Quarterly. Publish a data-backed market report (like [Flippa's quarterly reports](https://flippa.com/blog/digital-ma-market-trends-2026-q2/)) → break into 4–6 LinkedIn posts over 2–3 weeks, each extracting a different finding → offer the full report as a newsletter exclusive. Per the [thought leadership distribution playbook](https://www.flux.la/thought-leadership-strategy/): "One 'download our report' LinkedIn post earns almost no organic reach. Instead: a sequence of 4–6 individual posts over 2–3 weeks, each extracting a different finding or angle." **Confidence: high.**

### 6.3 The Contrarian Take Loop
**Hook:** "Everyone says [X]. The data says [Y]. Here's why."
**Distribution motion:** Identify a commonly held belief in the acquisition community → refute it with Eva's proprietary data → post on X → amplify on LinkedIn → write the full argument in the newsletter. This triggers the 86% of hidden decision-makers who "prefer perspectives that challenge their assumptions" ([MarketersIndex](https://marketersindex.com/redefining-b2b-thought-leadership-crafting-content-that-influences-executive-decisions/)).

### 6.4 The Build-in-Public Loop
**Hook:** "Eva's scoring model just got upgraded from v5 to v6. Here's what changed and why."
**Distribution motion:** Share the iteration → what the AI got wrong → how it was corrected → what it means for deal quality. This loops whenever the model improves (which should be continuous).

### 6.5 The Community Amplification Loop
**Hook:** Engage in ETA communities (Search Fund Coalition LinkedIn, Acquisition Entrepreneurs events, relevant subreddits) by answering questions and sharing insights — not promoting Eva directly.
**Distribution motion:** "One thoughtful post beats ten drive-by links" ([Needle](https://useneedle.net/blog/micro-saas-distribution-one-niche-three-communities)). Lurk, comment helpfully for 2 weeks, then share a relevant teardown when contextually appropriate. **Confidence: med** (framework from micro-SaaS distribution; applicable but not tested in acquisition niche specifically).

First credible inbound DM typically arrives at week 8; first attributed booked call at week 10 ([Forkoff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/founder-growth/founder-funnel-strategy), [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/linkedin-content-strategy-b2b-conversion-secrets-drive-alex-alleyne-xaxif)). **Set expectations accordingly — this is a 6–10 month compounding play, not a 30-day sprint.**

## 8. CTA Ladder — Comment → DM → Audit → Call → Offer

Based on the Founder Funnel OS Convert/Close stages and B2B conversion benchmarks:

### Stage 1: Comment (Awareness → Engagement)
**CTA:** "Drop a 🔥 if you want to see the full teardown" or "What's your take on [X]?" in LinkedIn/X posts.
**Goal:** Generate engagement signals that boost algorithmic reach and identify interested prospects. The KPI floor: 0.5% of audience takes a next-step action in a given month ([Forkoff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/founder-growth/founder-funnel-strategy)). **Confidence: high.**

### Stage 2: DM (Engagement → Qualification)
**CTA:** Reply to commenters with a specific, personalized message. "Saw your comment on the [deal teardown]. Are you actively evaluating [SaaS/ecommerce/agency] deals right now?"
**Goal:** Qualify intent. The Signal-stage KPI: 60% of replies within 90 days come from accounts that match the ICP ([Forkoff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/founder-growth/founder-funnel-strategy)). Target: at least 5 inbound DMs/week referencing specific content. **Confidence: med** (KPI from a single agency's framework).

### Stage 3: Free Deal Audit (Qualification → Consideration) — THE ENTRY OFFER
**CTA:** "Send me a listing you're looking at. I'll run it through Eva's 11-parameter model and send you a free deal audit — no strings."
**Goal:** This is Eva's **signature entry offer**. It's a low-friction, high-value deliverable that demonstrates the product's value before any ask. The conversion benchmark: 0.5% of total audience books a call or takes a next-step action per month — the audit is the mechanism that drives this ([Forkoff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/founder-growth/founder-funnel-strategy)). **Confidence: high.**

### Stage 4: Call (Consideration → Intent)
**CTA:** After delivering the audit, offer a 20-minute call to discuss the findings and how Eva's scanning + scoring could systematize their deal-sourcing process.
**Goal:** Move from "I got value from the free audit" to "I understand how Eva could work for me." **Confidence: high.**

### Stage 5: Offer (Intent → Closed)
**CTA:** Present Eva's ongoing scanning + scoring service. The offer: Eva continuously scans Flippa, Acquire.com, and Empire Flippers against the operator's buy box, scores listings on 11 parameters, and surfaces the 3 worth closing.
**Goal:** Convert the relationship into a recurring engagement (retainer or subscription). The Close-stage outcome benchmark from the Founder Funnel OS cohort: median 37% shorter deal cycle and 120% larger closed-won deal sizes vs. cold-sourced pipeline ([Forkoff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/founder-growth/founder-funnel-strategy)). **Confidence: med** (cohort data from one agency).

## 9. Do-Not-Say List — Claims to Avoid

### 9.1 Financial Advice / Guaranteed Returns
**Never say:** "Guaranteed returns," "risk-free," "you will make money," "this is a sure thing," or any language implying investment advice or guaranteed outcomes.

**Why:** The [SEC warns](https://mb.com.ph/2026/06/29/sec-warns-public-against-empire-x-astra-investment-schemes) that "any promise of guaranteed profits is a major red flag" and that "legitimate financial and investment products do not guarantee fixed or extremely high returns." While Eva is not selling securities, any language that could be construed as investment advice or guaranteed returns creates regulatory and reputational risk. **Confidence: high.**

### 9.2 Unsubstantiated Performance Claims
**Never say:** "Our scoring model is 100% accurate," "We've never been wrong," or any claim you cannot substantiate with data on demand.

**Why:** The [SEC Marketing Rule](https://regfin.com/blog/sec-marketing-rule) (Rule 206(4)-1) prohibits advertisements that "include a material statement of fact the adviser cannot substantiate upon demand." While Eva may not be a registered investment adviser, this is the standard the market and regulators apply to performance claims. Any claim must be backed by verifiable data. **Confidence: high.**

### 9.3 "Patentable" / IP Claims
**Never say:** "Our AI model is patentable," "We have proprietary technology no one can replicate," or any claim about IP protection that hasn't been vetted by legal counsel.

**Why:** Patent claims require legal opinion and carry specific regulatory implications. Overstating IP protection is a material misrepresentation. **Confidence: high.**

### 9.4 Misleading Statements
**Never say:** Anything that is "an untrue statement of material fact" or "omits a fact necessary to make the statement not misleading" per the [SEC Marketing Rule's first prohibition](https://regfin.com/blog/sec-marketing-rule). This includes:
- Cherry-picking successful deals without disclosing failures
- Presenting the 92-deal dataset as representative without disclosing selection criteria
- Implying that Eva's scoring guarantees a good acquisition outcome

**Confidence: high.**

### 9.5 Testimonials Without Disclosure
**Never use:** Paid or compensated testimonials without clear disclosure of the compensation arrangement.

**Why:** The [SEC Marketing Rule Prong Two](https://regfin.com/blog/sec-marketing-rule) covers "any testimonial or endorsement for which the adviser provides compensation" — and a paid promoter's post "is an advertisement under this prong even though the adviser didn't write it." Disclose all compensation. **Confidence: high.**

### 9.6 Generic Superlatives / Marketing Tells
**Avoid:** "Leading," "best-in-class," "world-class," "revolutionary," "game-changing," "in today's fast-paced world," or any unsubstantiated superlative.

**Why:** These are perceived as filler and undermine credibility. The [Edelman-LinkedIn research](https://marketersindex.com/redefining-b2b-thought-leadership-crafting-content-that-influences-executive-decisions/) found that "generic industry statistics, cited by every competitor, are perceived as filler." The internal review checklist recommends checking content is "free of unsubstantiated superlatives, vague 'leading' claims, embedded CTAs that break the editorial frame, and clichés." **Confidence: high.**

### 9.7 Disclaimers to Include
Every piece of content should include a brief disclaimer: "Eva Growth Agency provides deal-sourcing and scoring tools for informational purposes only. We do not provide investment, legal, or tax advice. All acquisition decisions involve risk. Past performance does not guarantee future results." **Confidence: high** (standard practice; `confidence:med` on exact wording — consult legal counsel).
