### SEC solicitation research for item 2 (initial pass)

Core fork is Reg D **506(b) vs 506(c)**:
- **506(b)** — no general solicitation/public advertising allowed at all.
  Requires a pre-existing substantive relationship with each investor before
  pitching. This is what most funds our size use.
- **506(c)** — DOES allow public advertising (LinkedIn posts qualify) but
  ONLY to accredited investors, and the issuer must take "reasonable steps"
  to VERIFY accreditation (not just self-attestation) — e.g. reviewing W-2s/
  tax returns/brokerage statements, third-party accreditation letter, or a
  high minimum check size ($200K individuals / $1M entities is SEC-blessed
  as one acceptable verification method) ([Morgan Lewis, 2025-03-20](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/new-sec-guidance-eases-burden-in-rule-506c-accredited-investor-verification-requirements)).
- Separately, SEC's Marketing Rule (206(4)-1, merged with the old
  solicitor's rule 206(4)-3) governs how a registered investment adviser can
  advertise/pay for referrals — testimonials/endorsements need disclosures
  ([Troutman Pepper, 2022-10-31](https://www.troutman.com/insights/the-secs-new-marketing-rule-practically-speaking-testimonials-and-endorsements/)).

**Implication for the Sai-network LinkedIn plan:** if Storeys' Fund I PPM is
filed under 506(b) (likely, if this is how it's been run so far), posting
publicly on LinkedIn to solicit is a general-solicitation violation —
switching to 506(c) is required first, which then obligates real
accreditation verification (not just "are you accredited?" checkbox) for
every investor who comes in through that channel. This needs fund counsel
confirmation before any post goes out — flagging as EVA-Compliance-Agent's
first real gate, not a green light yet.
